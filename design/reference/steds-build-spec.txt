5. RENDERING TIPS
1. Oak: 1px wavy vertical streaks of OAK[2] on OAK[3], with nested U/V arches every 30–60 px on panels; rail grain runs horizontal. Keep grain within 1.5 steps. Put 1px OAK_HI[2] on rail tops and 1px OAK[0] under the cap and panel heads. Never outline.
2. Keep walnut redder, darker and duller than oak. Posts stay plain. In sun it jumps to WALNUT_SUN orange; that warm-sun against cool-shade contrast is the room's signature.
3. Tile reflections are smeared mirror images with a Fresnel ramp, broken at seams. The window glare streak is cool, sun patches have white cores, and furniture legs make short blurry smears.
4. Navy is flat paint with no texture. Use 4x4 Bayer only where it changes step, and NAVY_SUN where sun hits it.
5. Plaster and ceiling are smooth. Dither bands at least 6 px wide, add 0.5 ft of corner occlusion, keep the ceiling whiter than the walls, and use '#b4b3aa' for shaded planes that face the window.
6. The window panes are the brightest pixels in frame. Bloom eats 1px into the bronze, frame faces that look up at the sky take '#3e5670', and the view stays low-contrast. Never draw the screen as a grid.
7. Every sunlit edge gets a 1px hot rim. Mullion bars show inside every sun patch. Keep the near corners dark so the eye goes to the window.
8. Brass is a dark-mid body with a 1–2 px BRASS[5] arc. The transom glows celadon. The picture rail is a 1px dark line plus a 1px light ledge. String lights are a 1px bulb with a 40% halo.
